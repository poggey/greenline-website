## 2. The state of safety on Australian sites

Let's start with the picture most safety documents skip past.

In 2024, **188 Australian workers did not come home from work** — each one a death from a traumatic, work-related injury. Construction accounted for **37 of those deaths (around one in five)**, sitting consistently among the small handful of industries that carry the heaviest toll. *(Source: Safe Work Australia, Key Work Health and Safety Statistics.)*

Two hazards dominate the construction story, and neither is exotic:

- **Falls from height.** This remains one of the leading killers on Australian worksites, and construction carries the bulk of it — in a recent year, nearly **half of all fall-from-height fatalities** happened in our industry. Worryingly, the national fall-from-height death toll jumped sharply — up around **71% in a single year** — a reminder that this is a live, present risk, not a solved problem.
- **Vehicles and moving plant.** Across all industries, vehicle incidents are the single largest cause of worker death — roughly **42% of fatalities**. On a busy site, the line between "traffic management plan" and "tragedy" is often a few metres and a moment's inattention.

> **◆ DID YOU KNOW?**
> Labourers, trades workers and machinery operators account for the overwhelming majority of fall-from-height deaths. The people most exposed are often the ones with the least say in how the work is set up — which is exactly why systems have to be designed *with* them, not *at* them.

And the harm isn't only physical. In 2023–24 there were roughly **146,700 serious workers' compensation claims** — each one a person off work for a week or more. The fastest-growing category is **psychological injury**. Mental health conditions now make up over one in ten serious claims, and they keep people away from work around **five times longer** than the average physical injury. Psychosocial hazards are no longer a "soft" issue — under model WHS laws they are a regulated duty.

That progress didn't come from luck or from thicker manuals. It came from businesses treating safety as something they *do*, every day, rather than something they *file*.

## 3. Why good intentions aren't enough: the compliance gap

Most businesses we meet are not careless. They have policies. They have a folder — sometimes an entire shelf — of documents. And yet the risk persists.

Why? Because there is a difference between **having a system** and **having a system people actually use.**

We call the gap between the two "shelf-ware": safety management systems written to satisfy an audit, full of language no one on site speaks, describing a way of working that doesn't match how the job is really done. Shelf-ware passes the prequalification check and then gathers dust. When something goes wrong, it offers no protection — to the worker, or to the directors who signed it off.

The other failure mode is the opposite extreme: **over-engineering.** A small contractor handed a 200-page system built for a tier-one principal will quietly ignore most of it, because complying fully would mean stopping work. Too much process fails for the same reason as too little — it doesn't fit the operation.

The goal is neither maximum paperwork nor minimum effort. It is **fit-for-purpose**: the right controls, written in plain language, sized to the business, owned by the people doing the work.

> **◆ A QUICK TEST**
> Pick any procedure in your safety system and ask a worker on site to explain it in their own words. If they can, it's a control. If they can't — or didn't know it existed — it's paperwork. The aim of every Greenline engagement is to turn the second kind into the first.

## 7. The bottom line: safety as a business advantage

Safety is sometimes framed as a cost centre — a tax on getting the job done. The evidence says the opposite.

Safe Work Australia's research found that if work-related injury and illness were eliminated, the national economy would be around **$28.6 billion larger**, with roughly **185,500 more full-time jobs** and an average wage uplift of about **1.3%**. At the level of a single business, the logic is the same: incidents cause delays, rework, insurance pain, reputational damage, lost tenders and devastated people. Prevention is almost always cheaper than the alternative.

There's a commercial edge, too. Strong, demonstrable HSE performance increasingly *wins work* — it's a prequalification gate on major projects and a genuine differentiator in competitive tenders. Done well, safety isn't what slows you down. It's part of what lets you compete.

> **◆ THE SHORT VERSION**
> Fewer incidents → fewer delays, lower premiums, better tender scores, and people who go home each night. Practical safety pays for itself.

## 9. Brand strategy & the big idea

### Positioning

> **Greenline HSE — practical Health, Safety & Environmental expertise for Australian construction, infrastructure and rural operations. Senior advice, delivered direct.**

**Personality, in five words:** grounded, experienced, plain-spoken, precise, human.

**What the brand is *not*:** corporate and faceless, fear-driven, jargon-heavy, template-generic, or trying too hard. No stock-photo handshakes. No clip-art ticks. No "synergy."

### Voice & tone

Plain English with quiet authority. Short sentences. Says the useful thing, not the impressive-sounding thing. Warm but never soft on the facts. The reader should feel they're hearing from an expert who respects their time and intelligence — the same voice that runs through this white paper.

### The big idea — "The Line"

The name does the heavy lifting. *Greenline* is a single, loaded word — a **line** — and the entire visual and motion language is built from it:

- **The safety line** — the boundary you don't cross; a clear line of sight; "drawing the line."
- **The set-out line** — the surveyor's string, the chalk snap, the precision of getting a build right from the first mark.
- **The horizon line** — where green rural land (her roots) meets built structure (her work). The brand literally sits on that line.
- **The through-line** — one experienced person connecting every phase, every sector.

**Design expression:** a single continuous line — in the signature Hi-Vis green — that *draws itself*, runs through the whole page, becomes a horizon, traces a path, underlines a word, then resolves into the logo. It is the one device that makes the site instantly recognisable and impossible to mistake for a template. Everything else is restraint; the line is the signature. **Because the line does so much of the work, several sections need no photography at all** — the line, the hairline grid, the line-icons and the hero numerals are the design.

## 10. Visual identity (the branding scheme)

### Logo

A confident lowercase/uppercase wordmark — **`greenline`** set in the display serif, with **`HSE`** in small mono caps, tracked out, sitting to the side or beneath.

- **Option A — The drawn line.** A thin Hi-Vis line enters from the left, runs *through* or *under* the wordmark like a set-out line, and continues off-frame. On load it animates: the line draws across, the word fades up behind it.
- **Option B — The horizon "g".** The bowl of the "g" is broken by a horizontal line — half the mark reads as farmland/horizon, half as structure. Subtle; rewards a second look.

Favicon / avatar: a single short Hi-Vis line segment on Limewash — minimal, ownable.

### Colour palette

**A light scheme with deliberate dark accents.** Warm Limewash paper carries most of the page; deep Field Green gives gravity and credibility; soft Sage adds quiet structure; one electric Hi-Vis accent — used *sparingly* — is the line and the call to action; and **Bitumen** is held in reserve for **type and one or two deliberately dark, high-contrast sections** (the statistics band, the closing run grounds in deep green).

High contrast is welcome — a dark band against all that light looks designed and gives the page rhythm. The rule is **occasional, not continual**: dark as a *feature*, never the default, and **never dark imagery** — photographs always stay bright.

| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Limewash** | `#F7F4EA` | Primary canvas — most of the page (never stark white) |
| **Cloud** | `#FCFAF3` | Lifted panels, cards, callouts |
| **Sage Mist** | `#E9F0E6` | Soft daylight tint for alternating bands |
| **Field Green** | `#2D5A3D` | Primary brand green; headings; buttons; closing band |
| **Sage** | `#8BA88E` | Hairlines, borders, quiet UI |
| **Bitumen** | `#18271D` | Text ink — and the one or two deliberate dark sections |
| **Hi-Vis** | `#C7E94B` | The signature line, focus states, key CTAs — use *rarely* |

Rule of thumb: roughly **65% Limewash/Cloud, 20% Field Green/Sage, ~10% deliberate dark, ~5% Hi-Vis.** Light dominates; one or two dark moments punctuate it; Hi-Vis only ever says "look here." *(Accessibility: Hi-Vis is a graphic/focus colour, not a text colour on light — set type in Field Green, Bitumen, or off-white on the dark sections.)*

*One cohesive palette throughout — the two areas of work are distinguished by content and layout, not by a separate colour theme.*

### Typography

A three-voice system that mirrors the brand — warmth + precision:

- **Display (headlines): `Fraunces`** — an editorial serif with character and a literary, slightly old-world warmth. Carries the heritage and trust; nods to the Irish roots. *(Alt: Newsreader, Spectral.)*
- **Body & UI: `Mona Sans` or `Hanken Grotesk`** — a clean, modern grotesque with more personality than the default Inter. Highly readable. *(Alt: Geist, Schibsted Grotesk.)*
- **Mono (labels, stats, section numbers, captions): `Geist Mono` or `Space Mono`** — evokes site documentation, survey readings and technical drawings. Used for the `01 / 02 / 03` section indices, stat figures, and small-caps labels.

Big, confident headline sizes. Generous line-height in body. The mono labels are a quiet signature — they make the whole thing feel engineered.

### Graphic language

- **Thin rule lines** everywhere — the page reads a little like a technical drawing or spec sheet, with hairline dividers and a faint structural grid on Limewash.
- **Mono section indices** (`01 — THE STATE OF SAFETY`) like a drawing register.
- **Generous whitespace and asymmetry** — confident, not cramped; never a wall of boxes.
- **Custom thin-line icons** (matching the line motif) — never generic icon-pack glyphs.
- **Stat callouts** treated as hero typography (the `188`, `42%`, `13.7` figures become design features, not footnotes — and the dark stat band makes them sing).
- **The line and shapes as content** — a section can be carried by the drawn line, a horizon rule or a quiet line-icon set; an image is a choice, not a requirement.

### Art direction (photography & imagery)

Bright, editorial and atmospheric — **luminous, never moody**, and never glossy stock. Imagery stays light even where the surrounding section is dark. Principles:

- **Honest over staged** — real sites and real texture, but shot in good light, not grit for its own sake.
- **Daylight to match the palette** — fresh greens, warm sand and clear morning or golden light; open skies, not overcast or storm.
- **Anonymous or distant figures** — backs turned, silhouettes, hands and boots — *no identifiable faces.* Supports the low-profile launch and looks more premium.
- **The rural ↔ built duality** as a recurring theme — horizons, the meeting of land and structure, under open sky.
- **Texture as content** — concrete, limewash, hi-vis fabric, survey paper — used full-bleed behind type, kept pale and clean.
- **Let the Line and type carry sections** — not every section needs a photo; the drawn line, hairline grids, line-icons and hero numerals are design in their own right.

## 11. Motion & interaction design (GSAP)

The site should *feel* engineered and alive — but with restraint. Motion earns its place; nothing bounces for the sake of it. **All motion is GSAP-driven.**

**Stack:** **GSAP** + **ScrollTrigger** (scroll-linked reveals and pins), **SplitText** (headline reveals), and **Lenis** for buttery smooth scroll. Light SVG for the line; optional small canvas touches. Everything wrapped in a `gsap.matchMedia()` so motion can be dialled down or off.

**Signature interactions (all GSAP):**

1. **The drawing line** — an SVG path in Hi-Vis, animated with GSAP `DrawSVGPlugin` (or a `strokeDashoffset` tween), that draws on load and continues to draw/branch as you scroll (ScrollTrigger scrub), threading the whole page together. The hero's defining moment.
2. **The horizon shift** — the hero opens on a bright green field and, scrubbed by ScrollTrigger, transitions across the horizon line into a sunlit built site (her story, told in motion).
3. **Counting stats** — the `188`, `42%`, `13.7` figures count up via a GSAP number tween when they scroll into view. High-impact on the dark stat band.
4. **Pinned horizontal scroll** — the Services line-icons pin and scroll sideways (ScrollTrigger pin + horizontal tween), turning the list into a journey along the line.
5. **Split-text headline reveals** — display lines mask-reveal word by word with SplitText as they enter.
6. **Clip-path section wipes** — GSAP transitions read as a line *passing across* the screen rather than a generic fade — ideal entering and leaving the dark band.
7. **Magnetic CTAs + custom cursor** — buttons subtly pull toward the pointer (GSAP quickTo); a minimal custom cursor that interacts with the line.
8. **Parallax imagery** — gentle GSAP/ScrollTrigger depth on full-bleed photography.

**Non-negotiables:** full `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks (everything must work and look good with motion off), a strict performance budget (lazy-load media, no jank on mobile), and keyboard-accessible focus states (the Hi-Vis accent doubles as the focus ring).

## 14. Build, SEO & credibility notes

Because the site exists chiefly to *look legitimate when someone searches*, the technical basics matter as much as the visuals:

- **Suggested stack:** Astro or Next.js (fast, SEO-friendly, static-deployable) with **GSAP + ScrollTrigger + Lenis** for motion. Host on Vercel/Netlify/Cloudflare Pages.
- **Findability & trust:** clean semantic HTML; descriptive page title and meta description; Open Graph/Twitter cards (so a shared link looks professional); and **schema.org structured data** — `ProfessionalService` for Greenline and `Person` for Catriona, listing her credentials. This is what helps Google present the firm credibly.
- **Performance:** image compression and lazy-loading, a Lighthouse score worth bragging about; motion must never cost speed, especially on mobile.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG-minded contrast (set type in Field Green, Bitumen, or off-white on dark bands — never Hi-Vis on light), full keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` support, alt text on every image.
- **Low-profile by design:** indexable and professional, but no hard-sell lead-gen — no pop-ups, no fake urgency. A single calm contact path. The site should feel like a confident business card, not a funnel.
- **Footer essentials:** ABN/business name, service area (Australia-wide), a single contact email, and the tagline. Quietly complete.
